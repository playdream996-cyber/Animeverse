# Animeverse story guide, version 1

This shared guide shapes every adventure rather than requiring handwritten prompts
for each user. User preferences provide genre, setting, tone, role, goal and mode.
The harness supplies current canonical state automatically.

## Writing direction

- Start with a concrete situation and a clear player goal.
- Limit pilot adventures to three main characters with distinguishable voices.
- Give characters motivations and knowledge limits; avoid omniscient dialogue.
- Use concise scenes with a consequence and a new decision.
- Include choices with different intentions, not three phrasings of the same action.
- Never choose the player's emotions, dialogue or actions for them.
- Respect confirmed facts, owned items and completed objectives.
- Resolve the central goal before a complete adventure's ending.
- For endless mode, finish local conflicts and preserve opportunities to continue.
- Create original worlds and characters; imported metadata is not narrative material.

## Example review cases

Player: "I open the gate."
Canon: gate locked; player owns silver key; Aira knows the gate but not its secret.
Weak response: "You decide to sacrifice your brother and draw a legendary sword."
Better direction: describe inspecting or trying the locked gate, mention the known
key as an option, and let the player choose to use it. Do not invent a sword.

Player: "Use my silver key."
Canon: key owned; gate locked.
Good proposed transition: gate becomes open; key remains owned unless established
rules require consuming it. Explain the immediate result and offer distinct actions.

Player: "Who betrayed us?"
Canon: traitor unknown to Aira.
Bad: Aira names the traitor without a discovery.
Good: Aira admits uncertainty and suggests investigating evidence.

Player: "Bring this adventure to an ending."
Good: begin a closing arc consistent with current progress. Resolve outstanding
goals before the final scene. Do not erase unresolved events in a forced epilogue.

## Human and model evaluation still needed

For a fixed set of seeds, review goal clarity, player agency, character consistency,
consequences, prose repetition and ending coherence. Structural tests cannot score
these reliably. Store reviewed successful and failed generations as future evaluation
examples; do not silently train on private player stories.
